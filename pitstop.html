The Problemthe counter, before

An Indian workshop runs on a paper diary, WhatsApp forwards and the owner’s memory. Estimates get argued at the counter, the customer cannot see what they are paying for, and the GST invoice comes from a typist shop two streets away. Software for garages exists, but it is built for dealership chains with a back office, not for one owner running the floor from a phone.

PitStop covers the full loop of a repair: intake, job card, mechanic assignment, customer approval, GST invoice, payment. Nothing more, and every screen assumes a phone, a hurry and grease.

What It Doesintake to invoice, nothing more

01

Job Cards, Intake to Gate Pass

Eight statuses from received to delivered, a mechanic on every card, and a chase list that surfaces whatever is stuck so the owner does not have to remember.

02

Customer Approval by Link

The customer gets a link, not an app. They see every line with a price and approve or decline from their own phone. The link is the credential, and it is built to be safe in hostile hands.

03

GST Invoicing Done Properly

CGST and SGST or IGST, decided by place of supply, rate wise summaries, gapless invoice serials and the amount in words. The tax document is correct, not approximately correct.

04

UPI on the Invoice

A QR on every invoice sends money straight into the garage’s own bank account. No gateway, no KYC paperwork, no cut. The owner watches it land in their banking app and marks it collected.

05

Old Parts Returned

The scrap goes back to the customer and the job card records it. A small feature, but it is the difference between a garage a customer trusts and one they don’t.

06

Workshop Chat with Voice Notes

Every job card is a thread tied to a vehicle. Mechanics send voice notes instead of typing, broadcasts reach the whole floor, and delivered cars leave the inbox on their own.

07

Purchase Requests with Guardrails

Anyone can ask for a part. The owner approves with an amount cap, a supplier and an expiry, and the system re-checks all three at ordering time. Trust, but verified in a trigger.

08

Reads the Bills Customers Bring In

A customer photographs an old bill from any garage. PitStop reads it once, and when that car comes back the mechanic sees prior work matched against the new complaint. Pads three weeks ago is a comeback. Pads three years ago is wear.

09

Two Markets, One Product

A single flag switches a garage between Indian GST and US sales tax, and it reaches all the way down: the maths, the invoice serials, the currency, the dates, even whether the odometer is in kilometers or miles.

10

Installs from the Browser

A PWA with an offline shell, not an app store listing. Zoom is deliberately left on, because a mechanic reading a part number in bad light needs to pinch.

The Deliberate Cutwhat I chose not to build

Chose

A UPI QR on the invoice

over integrating a payment gateway

A gateway needs a merchant account, company KYC and a bank account before a single rupee can move. The QR puts money in the garage's own account today. The owner watches it land in their banking app and marks it collected. Ninety percent of the value, none of the paperwork, and no reconciliation promise the product cannot keep.

Chose

A content-free realtime signal

over publishing message rows to subscribers

The obvious design puts message bodies on the wire and trusts row security to filter them. It failed its own probe: a member of a private thread heard nothing while broadcasts worked. One table carrying only a topic and a tenant now signals that something changed, and the server re-reads under the same policy that guards every other query.

Chose

SQL matching on customer bills

over letting a model read and act on them

Customers photograph bills from other garages. A model reads each one once, into a table. Nothing it extracts is ever trusted or acted on, and the matcher that surfaces prior work is plain SQL, so nothing written on a photographed page can steer the system.

Chose

A PWA that installs from the browser

over native apps in two stores

One codebase, an offline shell, and no review queue between a fix and the counter. Pinch zoom is deliberately left on, because a mechanic reading a part number in bad light needs to zoom, and a tidier layout is not worth taking that away.

06

What Isn’t Built Yetthe honest list

Blocked, not unwritten

WhatsApp messaging needs Meta Business verification, DLT registration and template approval. The code is the small part.

Deliberately absent

There is no automatic payment reconciliation. UPI collection does not confirm payment, and the product never claims it does.

Unproven

Bill reading has storage, policy and matching under test, but extraction has never run against a real upload in production.

Untestable here

Barcode scanning is buildable and specified. A camera cannot be exercised in a sandbox, so it waits for the floor.

07

Why It Is Built This Waythe counter decides

Constraint

The Phone Is the Office

The owner runs the shop from a phone at the counter. Every feature is designed there first, and the desktop version is the afterthought.

Trade

UPI Without a Gateway

A payment gateway needs merchant KYC and takes a cut. A UPI QR needs neither. Ninety percent of the value for none of the paperwork.

Belief

Why the Scrap Goes Back

Old parts returned, every line priced before work starts, an invoice that stands up to a tax officer. The software just makes honesty the cheap option.
