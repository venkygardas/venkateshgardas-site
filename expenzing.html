01

The work

Expenzing builds spend management and lending workflow software for Indian enterprises. My ground was the unglamorous middle of fintech: purchase requisitions, approval matrices, invoice matching, and the journey a loan application takes from a branch officer's desk to a sanction letter.

What the work actually taught me is that most of fintech is modelling somebody's policy document correctly the first time. A delegation of authority matrix looks simple in a PDF and becomes forty edge cases in production. Who approves when the amount crosses a band mid-flow? What happens when the approver is on leave and their delegate resigns? The product wins or loses on those questions, not on the dashboard.

02

What I took from it

01

The approval chain is the product

Users tolerate an ugly screen. They do not tolerate a purchase order stuck with no explanation. State, ownership and escalation were where all the real design happened.

02

Configuration beats customisation

Every enterprise believes its process is unique. Ninety percent of it is the same eight patterns wearing different names. Building the patterns once, configurable, was the difference between a product and a services shop.

03

Where PitStop comes from

Years of watching big companies buy workflow software made the gap obvious: the small business that needs it most can afford none of it. PitStop is that lesson, applied.
